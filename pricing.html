What shapes a sprint

What shapes the scope of a sprint.

A sprint is one workflow either way. The difference between a simple sprint and a complex one is complexity, not padding. These six factors account for nearly all of it.

Number of systems integrated

One well-documented API sits at the simple end. A CRM, a ticketing system, a data warehouse, and a legacy ERP with no sandbox sit at the complex end.

Data readiness

Clean, permissioned, retrievable content is quick to work with. Scattered documents with no access model, no ground truth, and no owner means we build that first.

Regulated or customer-facing surface

Internal drafting tools carry less burden than anything touching customers, money, health, or legal — those need tighter controls and heavier review.

Eval depth required

A representative eval set with a handful of dimensions is one level of effort. Adversarial cases, per-release regression gates, and human-rated judgment sets are another.

Approval gates and reviewers

Each human gate is a workflow of its own: queues, routing, escalation, SLAs, and an audit trail that survives inspection. More reviewers, more surface.

Change-management load

Five power users who want the tool need little. Two hundred people across three time zones who did not ask for it means training, rollout, and support design.

We price at the end of scoping, not before it. Significant scope changes are handled through change orders that adjust timeline, price, or both — agreed in writing before the work changes.

Retainers

Retainers are sized by hours.

You choose a monthly allocation and can flex it with two weeks' notice. Unused hours roll forward for up to two months, and on-call time comes out of the same allocation.

40 hours / month

60 hours / month

Accelerated roadmap. Multiple projects in parallel, deeper optimization work.

80 hours / month

Roughly half-time senior capacity. Several systems or projects in flight at once.

Inclusions

What's always included.

In every sprint

Architecture design. System build. Integration with your infrastructure. An eval suite with a representative test set. Approval gates and escalation paths. Audit logging and monitoring. A written runbook and rollback criteria. Production launch. Hands-on handover training. 30 days of post-launch support.

What's not included

Model and infrastructure spend, which you pay your providers directly. New licenses for systems you don't already own. Ongoing operation after the 30-day window — that's what the retainer is for. Net-new data cleanup at warehouse scale. Work outside the one scoped workflow, which becomes its own sprint rather than quiet scope creep.

You own the code, prompts, evals, and infrastructure-as-code. We sign your NDA before any scoping conversation and your DPA before any of your data is in scope — see security and data handling. More detail on the FAQ and across the three services.
